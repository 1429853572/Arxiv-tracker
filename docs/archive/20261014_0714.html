Hide and Seek: HIDE Concepts to SEEK explanations for black box models
Authors: Bhanu Tokas, Rahul Nair, Rockwell Jackson, Hannah Kerner
First: 2026-10-09T17:56:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:56:59+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Interpreting machine learning model predictions is a central challenge, particularly for black-box vision models. Concept-based attribution methods interpret models using human-understandable concepts. Existing concept-based methods can only interpret classification and regression models. They require access to model weights or large concept-annotated datasets, and most methods cannot provide image-level attribution. We propose Hide and Seek (HnS), a new method for concept-level attribution that does not require access to model weights or labeled concept datasets. HnS can interpret a wide range of vision models. Given a concept and image set, HnS retrieves concept-aligned images using a vision-language model, localizes the concept within each image, masks the localized region, and measures attribution from the change in the black-box model's output between the original and masked images. This enables faithful attribution by directly measuring the model's behavior and supporting local attributions. We validated HnS on CelebA and COCO-Stuff. Compared to existing methods, HnS showed better agreement with ground-truth attributions. We demonstrated the applicability of HnS on diverse tasks, including image captioning and satellite image segmentation, where no existing concept attribution method can be used. We provided our anonymous code in the supplementary material.

Make It Flow: Animating Water in 3D Gaussian Splatting Scenes
Authors: Kjell Keune, Florian Hahlbohm, Jannis Möller, Susana Castillo, Martin Eisemann, Marcus Magnor
First: 2026-10-09T17:08:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:08:02+00:00
Comments: Overview video at https://youtu.be/qf04D73kgrc
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Explicit 3D scene representations such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have recently enabled high-quality, real-time novel view synthesis. However, these methods assume static scenes, limiting realism in the presence of dynamic phenomena ubiquitous in real-world environments. While coherent motion (e.g., humans or vehicles) can be captured and reconstructed, high-frequency effects such as flowing water remain intractable under casual capture conditions, where dense multi-view, high-speed acquisition is not feasible. We present a method to animate water in static 3DGS scenes, enabling visually plausible motion without requiring dynamic capture or physical simulation. Our approach estimates 2D optical flow from reference images or model renderings and lifts it into a consistent 3D Eulerian motion field. We propose a cinemagraph-inspired algorithm that applies this motion field to deform Gaussian primitives, producing temporally seamless looping animations. The resulting representation retains real-time rendering performance, significantly enhances perceptual realism, and preserves free-viewpoint navigation within the scene. We evaluate our method on a newly captured dataset of in-the-wild waterfall scenes and demonstrate clear improvements over prior work in dynamic Gaussian splatting and 3D cinemagraph generation in visual quality and computational efficiency.

Vision2Code: A Multi-Domain Benchmark for Evaluating Image-to-Code Generation
Authors: Ajay Vikram Periasami, Junlin Wang, Bhuwan Dhingra
First: 2026-05-11T22:50:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T16:51:01+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://image2code.github.io/vision2code/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
We study whether vision-language models (VLMs) can reconstruct structured, graphics-based images by writing executable code to generate them. Existing benchmarks on image-to-code generation are generally limited in the domains they cover, rely on reference source code for evaluation, and do not provide metrics to quantify domain-specific issues. To address this, we introduce Vision2Code, a multi-domain, reference-code-free benchmark containing 539 test examples from 15 different datasets, spanning charts and plots, geometry, graphs, scientific diagrams, documents and 3D spatial scenes. Evaluation is done by executing the generated programs and comparing their rendered outputs to the source images using dataset- and domain-specific rubrics for a VLM judge. We find that the final scores obtained in this manner have substantially higher correlation with human ratings than those obtained via generic VLM rubrics or standard pixel-based, embedding-based or perceptual similarity metrics. We further demonstrate the reliability of our evaluation under repeated sampling from the VLM judges, varying rubric weights, and in the presence of controlled errors. Our results highlight the variability across domains of existing image-to-code generation models, with accuracy on charts and graphs being significantly higher than other domains. Lastly, we demonstrate the utility of our scoring method for rejection sampling of generated programs for fine-tuning, improving Qwen3.5-9B by 16% and Phi-4-MM by 28% compared to their base checkpoints. Our code and data are publicly available at https://image2code.github.io/vision2code/.

Studying Comics at Scale with Artificial Intelligence: The PAGE Framework and Three Studies of American Comics and Japanese Manga
Authors: Emilio Ferrara
First: 2026-10-09T16:49:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T16:49:29+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Comics are one of the great visual records of the twentieth century: hundreds of thousands of pages in which a mass readership saw its leaders, its wars and its heroes. Most of this record has never been studied as a whole; comics scholarship proceeds one book at a time. We argue that artificial intelligence, including generative models, can change this to the benefit of the medium itself. We propose PAGE, a framework that makes artificial intelligence an instrument for comics research. It parses every scan into panels, text and a bibliographic record; annotates each page with a vision-language model that follows a written codebook, as a human coder would; grounds samples of these readings in human annotation; and explains change across an archive, with estimates controlled for publisher and genre. We demonstrate the framework on 3,929 American comic books (1938--1956) and 87 volumes of Japanese manga, in three studies, each resting on a different capability. The first pairs text search with visual reading to recover how a mass medium portrayed political power. The second measures the page's visual form across an industry and recovers the hand of individual artists. The third follows who speaks across two comics traditions, checked against human annotation. Beyond research, the same instruments can make archives searchable by what is drawn, open comics to blind and low-vision readers, and help preserve fragile collections and credit uncredited artists. We outline a research agenda in which artificial intelligence strengthens the study, preservation and reach of the medium.

RoboMME-Interference: Benchmarking Robot Memory Under Interference
Authors: Soumil Rathi
First: 2026-06-21T05:06:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T16:43:07+00:00
Comments: 9 pages, 5 figures. Updated results; added subgoal-memory systems
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Robots deployed in realistic settings will accumulate experience across many sessions and tasks over their deployment. The robot's tasks may often require it to remember information from multiple sessions ago, making long-context robot memory important for real-world deployments. However, most robot-memory benchmarks today are based on single episodes or a short context. To measure how current robot memory systems perform on longer sessions with more distractions, we introduce RoboMME-Interference, a cross-session benchmark built on RoboMME (Dai et al., 2026). For each query episode, we construct a session history using the query's relevant prior demonstration followed by a controlled number of unrelated sessions, which we provide to the VLA as memory and measure accuracy. Running RoboMME's released memory-augmented $π_{0.5}$ variants unmodified through this benchmark, we find that while perceptual memory variants improve success when given the history without any distractors, they decay strongly and steadily as unrelated sessions accumulate. The subgoal variants, which read the history with a vision-language model and pass written subgoals to the policy, improve less at their best but hold more of that improvement as distractors accumulate. Adding a retrieval step to the strongest perceptual variant, which selects the section of history most visually similar to the robot's current view and passes only that section to the policy, restores its no-distractor success rate at every interference level. With this release, we emphasize the importance of long-context memory and robustness to interference and show that current systems largely fail on such capabilities. The project page, videos, code, and data are at https://robotmemorybench.com.

SpatialASM: Internalizing Active Mentalization for Spatial Reasoning
Authors: Yuhong Liu, Shenxi Wu, Junyi Chi, Beichen Zhang, Xuanlang Dai, Jiaqi Wang, Yuhang Zang, Dahua Lin
First: 2026-10-09T16:39:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T16:39:50+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
When solving complex spatial reasoning tasks, ordinary humans naturally revisit the scenes from task-relevant viewpoints and mentally reconstruct the spatial layouts from those viewpoints. But can Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) proactively think in the same way? We term this ability Active Spatial Mentalization (ASM), an intrinsic component of visual intelligence that remains substantially underdeveloped in current LVLMs. To bridge this gap, we introduce SpatialASM, a scalable framework to internalize ASM into LVLMs through training-time spatial simulation. SpatialASM employs an allocentric novel-view generator to ground hypothetical viewpoints in visual evidence and produces two complementary forms of supervision: i) explicit ASM trajectories that teach LVLMs when and how to adaptively invoke view planning and imagination during spatial reasoning, and ii) automatically-constructed perspective-taking tasks that further strengthens viewpoint-conditioned scene imagination. On seven benchmarks spanning relatively perception-oriented spatial understanding to more challenging tasks requiring multi-image, cross-view and viewpoint-transformation reasoning, SpatialASM yields performance gain on LVLMs of four LVLM backbones in spatial reasoning while retaining their original general vision capabilities. The experimental results demonstrate that our approach improves average accuracy across the seven spatial benchmarks. And the synthesis of ASM trajectories provides a scalable pathway to alleviate the scarcity of spatial Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision for eliciting active viewpoint exploration and mental scene simulation.

OccludeBench: Can VLMs Find What's Missing?
Authors: Jasin Cekinmez, Addison J. Wu, Ryo Mitsuhashi, Linrong Cai, Xingyu Fu, Zhuang Liu
First: 2026-10-09T16:04:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T16:04:54+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
How well can vision-language models (VLMs) reason about what is missing from an image? We study this question with OccludeBench, a manually curated benchmark for recovering fully occluded objects from the visual evidence that remains. OccludeBench distinguishes direct cases, where the hidden object must be recovered from a specific physical cue such as a shadow or reflection, from inferential cases, where it can be inferred from broader scene context. Across five VLMs, accuracy averages 42.7% on direct cases versus 62.7% on inferential cases, exhibiting a 20.0-point gap. Humans attain higher accuracies of 63.4% versus 68.5% for direct and inferential images, respectively, with no significant gap. To locate this failure, we introduce OccludeTrace, a four-stage diagnostic that isolates where the visual reasoning process breaks down. The bottleneck emerges when models must recover the relevant object from the projection within the full image: they succeed on only 50% of failed examples, compared with 91% of examples they answer correctly. Extended reasoning, tool use, and few-shot prompting yield inconsistent improvements and do not reliably eliminate the gap. In contrast, an inpaint-then-confront intervention, which prompts models to first generate a candidate hidden object and then evaluate that hypothesis against the visible physical cue, reduces the gap to a statistically non-significant difference for both models with image-generation capabilities. Together, our results establish a tractable, controlled diagnostic for isolating how VLMs reason about visual content that is missing from an image.

How well can vision-language models (VLMs) reason about what is missing from an image?

SimVTA: Simulation-Grounded Video-Trajectory Alignment for Driving World Action Models
Authors: Changhui Jing, Haochen Tian, Hongchen Li, Tianyu Li, Caojun Wang, Zihao Zhang, Yanhao Liu, Chengen Xie, Bailin Li, Hao Zhao, Zhenjie Yang
First: 2026-10-09T16:01:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T16:01:09+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://opendrivelab.com/SimVTA/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Driving world action models (WAMs) learn from paired trajectories and future observations. Post-training with reinforcement learning allows the policy to explore alternative trajectories and improve planning using driving rewards. However, driving logs contain future videos only for the recorded trajectories, leaving these alternatives without corresponding visual supervision. Thus, we introduce SimVTA, a simulation-grounded post-training framework that combines trajectory optimization with video supervision. First, we sample candidate trajectories for each observation and use a real-world simulator (e.g., World Engine) to render future videos for high-reward candidates in real-world scenes reconstructed with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). This provides trajectory-matched supervision independent of the model's own predictions. Under a limited rendering budget, we prioritize video supervision for higher-error futures among these rendered candidates. Finally, we optimize trajectory planning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and use a flow-matching loss on the matched videos to update shared representations. At inference time, the model predicts trajectories directly, without simulator calls. On NAVSIM v1, applying SimVTA to a WAM initialization improves PDMS from 86.2 to 91.3 (+5.1). It achieves 88.2 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2 and an average HD-Score of 38.8 in closed-loop evaluation on HUGSIM. Ablations show that updating shared representations with video supervision improves planning, while prioritizing higher-error futures among high-reward trajectories further improves performance.

Abstract

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
In test and evaluation (T&E) of AI models, evaluators typically have access only to a held-out test dataset and no knowledge of the training data or its semantic composition. If critical input conditions---such as time-of-day, weather, or scene location---are underrepresented in this test dataset, T&E results can be misleadingly due to this semantic coverage gap. We propose a three-stage generative AI pipeline to detect and fill such semantic gaps without requiring ground-truth labels of these semantics. First, vision-language models (VLMs) perform zero-shot binary labeling of semantic factors, and an embedding-space clustering step identifies which semantics most affect downstream model behavior. Second, diffusion-based image-to-image models transform existing covered images into the underrepresented semantic domain, preserving scene content while augmenting high-level appearance. Third, statistical analysis of the augmented image embeddings verifies that the synthetic imagery improves dataset coverage before it is incorporated. Crucially, the synthetic data is used only to enrich the test set and no model retraining is performed for T&E. We validate the full pipeline on both image classification and object detection under day/night semantic gaps, showing that the augmented test datasets better distinguish models with and without exposure to the missing semantic, thereby yielding T&E conclusions that more closely match those of an ideal, fully covered dataset. Abstract
Every evaluation run of a vision-language model (VLM) already produces per-question artifacts (predictions, judged correctness, metadata) that leaderboards reduce to a single score and then discard. We reuse those artifacts to audit the benchmark itself, and add an audit layer to VLMEvalKit: six detectors that run as post-processing over saved outputs, plus one additional text-only rerun with visual inputs removed, emitting a per-question report. The layer reports per-question findings together with aggregate audit statistics; for the headline quantities, it attaches permutation-null excess where applicable, interval estimates for proportions, and sensitivity to the model pool. Auditing MMStar, a benchmark curated specifically so that the image is indispensable, with three models, we find zero aggregate accuracy gain from the image on 20.2% of its questions. Replacing the LLM judge with exact string matching changes no conclusion, and every scoring discrepancy between the two traces to one model whose free-form answers a parser cannot read. On a binary yes/no benchmark the detectors measurably fall out of calibration, a scope boundary that we measure and report. The audit layer, the prediction artifacts and the generated reports accompany this submission.

Efficient Confidence-Aware Densification for 3DGS
Authors: Manuel Hofer, Thomas Köhler, Felix Windisch, Bernhard Kerbl, Markus Steinberger, Lukas Radl
First: 2026-10-09T12:59:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T12:59:04+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
3DGS-MCMC has replaced the gradient-driven density control of 3D Gaussian Splatting with opacity-based sampling and relocation, and is now the de facto standard for prior-free densification under a fixed primitive budget. Because opacity stays high where the scene is already well reconstructed, this sampling sends most new primitives to the foreground and leaves the background under-reconstructed. At low budgets, prior-based methods such as ConeGS and EDGS outperform it, at the cost of a pre-trained radiance field or dense image matches and a separate initialization stage. We argue for a comeback of error-driven densification within the fixed-budget MCMC framework. Our confidence-aware densification keeps the fixed budget and gradual growth of 3DGS-MCMC, but places primitives by the image error instead of the opacity. New primitives are drawn mostly where screen-space gradients are large. We favor primitives with a low learned confidence, which marks regions the reconstruction still explains poorly, and relocate the primitives that contribute least to the rendered images to these places. Averaged over 24 scenes, our method outperforms 3DGS-MCMC in PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS at every budget from 100k to 2M primitives. At 100k, without a prior or an initialization stage, it recovers 0.84dB of the 1.05dB PSNR by which ConeGS leads 3DGS-MCMC, and from 500k on, it has the highest PSNR and SSIM of all fixed-budget methods we compare with, including ConeGS and EDGS. While inherently prior-free, our densification can also exploit priors: from the ConeGS or EDGS initialization, it matches ConeGS in PSNR and SSIM at 100k and is best in all three metrics at 500k.

Authors: Nikolaos Tsagkas, Andreas Sochopoulos, Chris Xiaoxuan Lu, Oisin Mac Aodha, Alexandros Kouris

Abstract
Deploying generalist robotic agents in the real world requires transferable skills. Specifically, a policy trained to clone a behavior from object-specific demonstrations must generalize beyond that object, otherwise data collection requirements become intractable. Recently, fine-tuning of pre-trained billion-parameter Vision-Language Models (VLMs), initially on large-scale robot datasets and then on fewer scenario-specific demonstrations, has emerged as the predominant paradigm for designing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. While these policies achieve state-of-the-art manipulation performance in-distribution, they remain brittle to minor spatial, semantic, and task variations. In this work, we address the inability of current models to decouple the declarative (i.e., concepts and entity semantics) from the procedural knowledge (i.e., how to do something) encoded in their parameters, which is a fundamental bottleneck for zero-shot skill transfer to novel objects. Unlike popular, state-of-the-art VLAs, we show that our modular approach successfully decouples knowledge representations, enabling robust behavior cloning and unprecedented zero-shot skill transfer capabilities across dissimilar, unseen objects.

SplatQ: Interaction-Aware Pruning of 3D Gaussian Splatting via Quadratic Selection
Authors: Hongbin Ye, Haixu Liu, Shenwei Kang, Wenxiu Wei, Tinglan Wang, Hongdong Zhu, Fan Tang, Qi Gao, Yin Ma, Kai Wen, Hai Wei, Nuo Qian, Wei-Shi Zheng, Shaobo Chen, Fa-Ting Hong, Jingke Meng

First: 2026-10-09T12:30:13+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T12:30:13+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) pruning methods primarily rely on global Top-K selection based on independent importance scores, lacking regional budget control and ignoring joint deletion effects. Consequently, they cannot ensure balanced reconstruction quality across a scene. We propose SplatQ, a combinatorial pruning algorithm that coordinates regional budget allocation and joint Gaussian selection under a fixed budget. SplatQ partitions Gaussians by spatial proximity and appearance similarity into size-limited blocks, decomposing global selection into subproblems of a specific size. It then smooths importance-based block retention quotas to prevent excessive budget concentration. Finally, it constructs a quadratic approximation of joint deletion effects using the responses of representative pixel colors to changes in Gaussian opacity. Individual responses, pairwise interactions, and quota penalties are incorporated into a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) objective, solved using simulated annealing (SA) or a coherent Ising machine (CIM) to jointly select Gaussians. Experiments on 5 benchmarks demonstrate that SplatQ outperforms state-of-the-art pruning methods in aggregate reconstruction quality, spatial quality balance, and rendering speed. Ablations confirm that budget smoothing improves local detail reconstruction, while interaction modeling improves Gaussian selection quality under a fixed budget.
Summary / 总结

Abstract
Agricultural visual models are typically evaluated for either recognition or localization, but reliable diagnosis requires identifying what is present and localizing the evidence. Agricultural visual question answering (VQA) datasets carry rich semantic labels but rarely link them to image regions, and adding such annotations by hand is costly at scale. We introduce AgroGround, a large-scale dataset for grounded agricultural recognition: identifying plant diseases and other agricultural targets and localizing their image regions. An automated pipeline converts the labels of eight agricultural VQA datasets into annotations for disease lesions and whole objects, producing 794,850 instruction examples. Healthy images provide negative supervision for disease queries, teaching the model to return empty predictions. We fine-tune a shared vision-language model on known-target grounding instructions combined with instructions requiring both recognition and localization. Healthy negatives raise abstention on healthy images to 95.0\%, and reinforcement learning improves lesion-level grounding. The resulting 2B model exceeds its annotation teacher in grounding F1 on our benchmark and on the external PlantSeg test set. AgroGround establishes a benchmark for grounded agricultural recognition, measuring joint correctness of identity and localization along with abstention on healthy images. Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-05-07T23:03:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T10:42:26+00:00
Comments: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision Language Models (VLMs) offer the exciting possibility of processing text as rendered images, bypassing the need for tokenizing the text into long token sequences. Since VLM image encoders map fixed-size images to a fixed number of visual tokens, varying rendering resolution provides a fine-grained compression knob. However, accuracy deteriorates quickly as compression increases: characters shrink below the vision encoder's effective resolution, making them indistinguishable. To address this, we propose LensVLM, an inference framework and post-training recipe that enables VLMs to scan compressed images, then selectively expand only the relevant images to their uncompressed form via learned tools. Building on Qwen3.5-9B-Base, LensVLM maintains accuracy comparable to the full-text upper bound at 4.3$\times$ effective compression and outperforms retrieval-based, text- and visual-compression baselines up to 10.1$\times$ effective compression across seven text QA benchmarks. LensVLM also generalizes to multimodal document and code understanding tasks, with the accuracy gain over baselines growing as compression increases. Our analysis validates this approach: training makes visual compression robust to rendering choices, and as compression grows the model increasingly relies on expanded content rather than unreliable visual reading. The analysis also yields practical tool-choice guidance: text expansion is preferable for rendered text, while high-resolution image expansion suits native documents whose layout cues carry task-relevant information.

Vision Language Models (VLMs) offer the exciting possibility of processing text as rendered images, bypassing the need for tokenizing the text into long token sequences.
COSY: Compositional 3DGS Synthesis for Disentangled Human Head Editing
Authors: Florian Barthel, Shalini De Mello, Koki Nagano, Wieland Morgenstern, Anna Hilsmann, Peter Eisert
Venue: ECCV 2026
First: 2026-05-22T18:22:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T10:19:44+00:00

Abstract
Recent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) GANs for human heads synthesize and render photorealistic 3D models in real-time and offer a vast variety in identity and appearance. However, controlling specific semantic attributes such as hair color or glasses remains challenging, as edits in the entangled latent space often induce unintended changes in identity or appearance. Although there are several methods that aim to disentangle the latent space post training by estimating directions that only modify certain features, these methods cannot guarantee complete disentanglement and often require pre-trained classifiers. In our approach, we propose a new generator architecture that synthesizes components, such as hair, skin, glasses, and torso, completely independently. This allows for changing the latent vector for one region while keeping the remaining parts fixed. Further, we achieve this separation using only sparse information such as the hair or skin color, eliminating the requirement of segmentation masks or geometric priors, often seen in prior work. To ensure matching shape and lighting conditions during editing, we allow minimal shared information via context tokens between the independent generators. These tokens even allow us to control the shape and light, without any prior annotation. Compared to existing works on GAN-based generation and editing, our method shows better disentanglement, more precise editing control, and competitive visual quality.

NovaSplat: Model-Free 6D Object Pose Estimation via 3D Gaussian Splatting Onboarding and Refinement

Abstract
6D pose estimation of novel objects without CAD models remains an open challenge: model-free methods must build the object representation from scratch during onboarding and have long trailed their model-based counterparts. We present NovaSplat, a model-free pipeline that closes this gap. From a short smartphone video captured at onboarding, we train a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) model that serves both as a template source for detection and coarse pose estimation and as a differentiable backbone for pose refinement. Our refiner optimizes the object-to-camera pose with combined photometric and geometric losses under a continuous rotation parametrization free of gradient singularities. On a household-object benchmark, NovaSplat surpasses all model-based methods. On a challenging industrial benchmark with textureless and specular surfaces, it outperforms all model-free competitors while substantially narrowing the gap to model-based ones. We further introduce NovaSplat-Lite, an inference variant that is $3.1\times$ faster at negligible accuracy cost. Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) exhibit complementary capabilities, yet selecting the most suitable expert for a given image-question query remains challenging due to heterogeneous multimodal inputs and varying expert strengths. Existing routing approaches rely on text-based cues or static model profiles, failing to capture fine-grained visual evidence and expert-specific failure patterns. To address this, we propose MHR-Router, a multimodal heterogeneous retrieval-enhanced adaptive routing framework for VLM inference. It accurately dispatches each multimodal query to the most suitable VLM expert via local retrieval evidence. Experiments show that MHR-Router effectively achieves heterogeneous modality routing and consistently improves accuracy over traditional baselines.

Abstract
Person re-identification is typically addressed by learning identity-discriminative embeddings from individual person crops. However, player re-identification in broadcast sports videos presents a unique challenge: players from the same team often share similar appearances, while video sequences provide repeated observations of the same players under different viewpoints and surrounding contexts. Existing crop-based approaches largely ignore this contextual information, which can be critical for resolving visually similar identities. Here, we propose ContextReID, a contextualized embedding framework that represents a player by jointly considering other players in the same frame and observations from different frames of the broadcast sequence. By incorporating both co-occurring players and cross-frame observations, the model learns identity features relative to the surrounding visual context rather than relying only on isolated appearance cues. We implement this framework with a vision-language model that takes sets of player crops as input and generates context-aware embeddings for individual players. A combined contextual learning and retrieval objective encourages the model to capture cross-frame identity relationships while maintaining discriminative embeddings for player matching. Experiments across three multi-object tracking benchmarks show that ContextReID improves player retrieval over conventional single-crop embedding approaches in both Rank-1 and mAP. These results highlight the utility of spatial and temporal context for reliable player identification in broadcast sports videos.

CompactSplat: Spatially Adaptive Gaussian Distribution for Feedforward Scene Reconstruction
Authors: Bing He, Jingnan Gao, Yunuo Chen, Zhengxue Cheng, Rong Xie, Li Song, Wenjun Zhang

Abstract
Reconstructing 3D scenes from sparse views without per-scene optimization remains highly challenging, especially for recovering accurate geometry and fine textures. While recent feedforward approaches leverage generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for scene generation, they typically assign one or multiple Gaussians to each pixel. Such uniform allocation not only produces highly redundant representations by wasting primitives in homogeneous areas, but also fails to exploit the inherent geometric priors of planar surfaces, often resulting in sub-optimal structural modeling. To address these limitations, we present CompactSplat, a novel feedforward framework that enables a content-aware allocation of 3D Gaussians. By integrating texture-guided spatial partitioning with hierarchical sampling, CompactSplat adaptively distributes primitives - concentrating dense Gaussians in complex regions while tiling flat areas with expanded, scale-modulated primitives. Moreover, our framework inherently supports dynamic sparsity control at inference time, enabling seamless trade-offs between rendering fidelity and memory footprint without requiring network retraining. Extensive experiments on RealEstate10K, DL3DV, and ScanNet demonstrate that CompactSplat consistently outperforms prior methods on both standard metrics and high-resolution rendering consistency, achieving high-fidelity reconstructions with significantly fewer primitives.

Abstract
Image-based 3D reconstruction provides a scalable representation for building inspection and maintenance. Abstract
Attention heads that attend to image regions relevant to a question are increasingly used to steer, ground and interpret vision-language models (VLMs) and to reduce their hallucinations. They are mostly found by ranking heads with an attention- pattern score and validated by steering or masking them. We ask how the choices inside such a discovery score shape the heads it returns, and which of the two validations identifies them. On Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct we vary the query type, the discovery corpus and the token positions whose attention is read (the attention source) in a full 2 x 3 x 2 design, and test all 12 head-sets for steerability (CERS: Contrastive Evidence-Region Steering score) and necessity (NCS: Necessity Con- trast Score) against layer-matched random head-sets. (1) The attention source and the query type reshape the selected heads far more than the corpus: changing the source keeps a median of 49 of the top-100 heads, the query 55 and the cor- pus 84, while two halves of the same data share 96-99. (2) Necessity follows the attention source: head-sets discovered from query-span attention are more nec- essary than last-token ones (difference 0.083, p=6 x 10^-10), and the two re- ferring + query-span sets lie 17-19 standard deviations above 250 layer-matched random sets (Holm-adjusted p=0.048). The referring + query-span effect re- plicates on several recent VLMs. (3) Steerability is not specific to the discovered heads: no discovered head-set steers better than its layer-matched random sets (rank p >= 0.14), and random sets drawn from the same or from any layer span the discovered sets' scores. Necessity therefore separates the discovery settings, while steering at this strength does not. We turn these findings into a protocol for reporting visual-head discoveries.

Volume Rendering with Metric--Phase Fields for Thin Structures and Sign-Ambiguous Geometry
Authors: Junkai Deng, Jiayi Kong, Xuhui Chen, Guanli Hou, Fei Hou, Junhui Hou, Wenping Wang, Ying He
First: 2026-10-09T06:40:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T06:40:44+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Neural implicit surface methods that couple volume rendering with signed distance functions (SDFs), such as VolSDF and NeuS, achieve high-quality multi-view reconstruction for watertight objects. However, these approaches rely on a globally consistent inside-outside partition of space. This assumption becomes fragile for thin structures (e.g., sheets, tubes, and thin walls) and can break down for sign-ambiguous geometry (e.g., surfaces with boundary, non-manifold junctions, and closely spaced surface components), often resulting in spurious closures or loss of fine structures. Unsigned distance field (UDF) formulations relax the global sign requirement and better support non-watertight geometry, but are substantially harder to optimize. We propose PhaseSurf, a neural volume rendering framework built on Metric-Phase Fields (MPFs). MPFs decompose geometry into a nonnegative metric field capturing unsigned proximity and a continuous phase field providing a soft sign cue. By coupling the two through residual phase injection, MPFs yield a signed composite implicit function with stable near-surface gradients while retaining UDF-like robustness in sign-ambiguous regions. This design enables direct iso-surface extraction using standard Marching Cubes and avoids the failure modes of both pure SDF- and UDF-based formulations. We further introduce MPF-specific training losses that enforce metric consistency, stabilize phase transitions near the interface, and suppress spurious geometry in free space. Experiments on multi-view datasets with thin structures and sign-ambiguous geometry show that PhaseSurf produces more stable reconstructions and higher-quality geometry than UDF-based methods and recent 3DGS pipelines, while avoiding artifacts typical of SDF-based approaches.
Summary / 总结
Neural implicit surface methods that couple volume rendering with signed distance functions (SDFs), such as VolSDF and NeuS, achieve high-quality multi-view reconstruction for watertight objects.

Abstract
Multi-view representations enable pretrained image and video vision-language models to understand 3D scenes, but their long visual token sequences make inference costly. Redundancy arises both from repeated observations of the same scene regions across views and from processing all visual tokens at every LLM layer, regardless of their relevance to the current computation. We propose GeoGR, a two-stage framework that jointly compresses scene representations and visual computation. At the projector level, GeoSemZip consolidates cross-view observations into 3D voxel tokens and combines semantic saliency with spatial coverage to construct a compact scene representation. Unselected voxel features are aggregated into contextual anchors to retain scene information that would otherwise be discarded. At the LLM level, we identify an effective visual computation window and observe that query-conditioned token relevance remains locally stable but changes over longer layer intervals. Guided by these findings, GroupRoute combines late entry and early exit with recoverable group-wise skipping. Low-relevance tokens bypass complete LLM layers while retaining their hidden states and can rejoin the active group at subsequent anchor layers selected under a computation budget. For compression-aware post-training, we construct a compact dataset of 67K samples by retaining 30% of the original training pool. With this protocol, GeoGR retains 95.2% and 96.8% of the dense post-trained models' performance on LLaVA-OneVision-7B and Video-3D LLM-7B, respectively, while accelerating LLM prefill by $6.8\times$ and $6.1\times$. These results demonstrate the consistent effectiveness of GeoGR across 3D VLMs with and without explicit 3D spatial encoding, highlighting a favorable performance-efficiency trade-off. The code is available at https://github.com/EIT-NLP/GeoGR.

Abstract
We introduce MVGraph, a benchmark for evaluating large vision-language models (LVLMs) on multi-graph reasoning where the evidence required by a query is distributed across multiple interrelated visual graph views. Although such evidence commonly resides in source-specific graphs, temporal snapshots, or local observations, existing graph reasoning benchmarks remain confined to a single self-contained structure, assessing only graph perception and reasoning while leaving the alignment and composition of evidence across views underexplored. MVGraph comprises 8k instances spanning 8 \textit{graph-theoretic} tasks, abstracted from two multi-graph scenarios (partial and temporal graph views). Each problem is programmatically constructed and verified to be unsolvable from a single view. Beyond end-to-end evaluation, we decompose multi-graph reasoning into four complementary capabilities---Perception, Alignment, Composition, and Reasoning---and design 29 diagnostic tasks with 64k instances to probe each one, along with golden intermediate representations for evaluating the intermediate process. Across 15 LVLMs, we reveal the difficulty of multi-graph reasoning and identify cross-view edge alignment and relation-level composition as prominent bottlenecks through stage-oriented diagnostics. Meanwhile, temporal constraints and increasing view counts expose further capability boundaries. Code and data are available at https://github.com/AAAndy-Zhu/MVGraph.

In-Context Learning Can Help Vision-Language Models Overcome Training Priors
Authors: Kun Wang, Xindi Wu, Sanghyuk Chun, Olga Russakovsky, Esin Tureci
First: 2026-10-09T03:40:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T03:40:07+00:00

Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) learn strong statistical regularities during training, which can make them fail to properly respond to visual evidence when input images violate those regularities. It is challenging to disambiguate these prior-induced errors from errors caused by the model missing the underlying visual capabilities. In this work, we use controlled visual in-context learning to encourage the model to focus on the visual evidence, and demonstrate that this alone is frequently sufficient to correct the errors -- suggesting that the models in fact do possess the relevant visual capabilities. Specifically, we show that across five vision-centric tasks, demonstrations yield large gains on prior-conflicting counterfactual examples ($+27.2\%$ for Qwen3-VL 235B and $+15.1\%$ for Qwen3-VL 32B) while leaving accuracy on prior-aligned examples nearly unchanged. These findings generalize across five VLM families with gains from $+9.7\%$ to $+30.3\%$. We analyze visual representations and find that demonstrations indeed redirect attention toward grounding cues while reducing reliance on cues that support the learned prior. Finally, we find that in-context learning is limited when the underlying visual capability is weak or absent. Together, these results show that current VLMs possess visual grounding capabilities that standard evaluations may overlook, underscoring the need for controlled evaluation design in diagnosing and improving model capability.
